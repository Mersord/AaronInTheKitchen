About these measurements

Metric is the default. US cups are 236.588 mL, not 250 mL. Flour, sugar and other supported dry ingredients use ingredient-specific weight conversions; they are not treated as water. Displayed conversions are rounded.

Where a reliable weight-to-volume conversion is unavailable, weighed ingredients use ounces or pounds in the US view; spoon-measured ingredients use millilitres in the metric view. Ounces (oz) mean weight, not fluid ounces. Counts, pinches, and “to taste” remain unchanged.

For baking and very small quantities, a kitchen scale is more reliable than estimated cups. Changing units does not adjust the recipe for a fan oven or a different pan.

Ingredient conversion reference · Conversion notes

The Base & Vegetables

  • Butter: 250 g (7:58)
  • All-purpose flour: 120 g (8:30)
  • Onion: 1 large (rough chopped) (1:30)
  • Celery stalks: 2 stalks (or approx. 500 g, rough chopped) (1:39)
  • Garlic: 1 whole head (pressed) (9:20)

Meat & Liquids

  • Chicken: 1 whole chicken (Aaron specifically uses 4 legs and 4 thighs with bones left on) (7:12)
  • Sausage: Traditional Andouille or local alternative (cut diagonally) (3:17)
  • Chicken stock: 2.366 L (added incrementally) (15:18)

Seasonings & Spices

  • Chicken bouillon cube: 1 cube (16:46)
  • Creole seasoning (e.g., Tony Chachere's): 59 mL (17:24)
  • Slap Ya Mama Cajun seasoning: 59 mL (17:17)
  • Garlic powder: 29.5 mL (16:46)
  • Onion powder: 29.5 mL (16:46)
  • Garlic flakes: 29.5 mL (16:31)
  • White pepper: 29.5 mL (16:46)
  • Red chili flakes: 29.5 mL (16:22)
  • Steak seasoning (or all-purpose seasoning): 29.5 mL (17:06)
  • Sea salt: A pinch (17:06) 

(Note: Aaron starts with half quantities of the seasonings during the video, tastes it, and then adds 15 mL of everything to boost the liquid's final flavor profile (17:45)).

Step-by-Step Cooking Instructions

Step 1: Chop the Veggies and Meat

Give a very rough hand-chop to your onion and celery stalks so they leave thick, rustic chunks in your gumbo (0:13). Slice your sausages at a diagonal angle (4:52). Prepare your chicken pieces by separating the legs and thighs, keeping the bones intact for extra flavor (7:12).

Step 2: Cook the Roux

Turn on your Instant Pot's high-heat sauté function (5:25). Melt 250 g of butter and stir in 120 g of all-purpose flour (7:58). Stand over the pot and stir constantly for roughly 7 minutes until the mixture turns into a rich, chocolatey golden-brown color (5:33). Do not walk away, or it will burn (9:26)!

Step 3: Deglaze with Vegetables

Carefully dump your chopped onion and celery directly into the hot roux (12:28). The natural moisture and acids from the vegetables will react with the oil and safely deglaze any flour clinging to the bottom of the pot (11:22).

Step 4: Build the Liquid Base

Pour in 473 mL of your chicken stock to keep the vegetables cooking down and lower the heat (13:26). Use a garlic press to crush a whole head of garlic directly into the mix (9:20).

Step 5: Add the Proteins

Drop your bone-in chicken pieces and sliced sausages straight into the pot (14:59). Pour in the remaining 1.893 L of chicken stock, making sure to scrape the bottom to ensure nothing sticks (15:18).

Step 6: Season Heavily

Add your bouillon cube, Cajun seasonings, garlic powder, onion powder, garlic flakes, white pepper, red chili flakes, steak seasoning, and sea salt (16:31). Stir deeply to make sure the seasoning completely incorporates into the liquid (17:34).

Step 7: Pressure Cook

Secure the lid of your Instant Pot (18:14). Cook under pressure for 30 to 35 minutes (18:28). This rapid-cooking step locks in all the deep flavors that traditionally take hours on a stove (5:51).

Step 8: Serve

Carefully vent and open the Instant Pot (18:50). Serve hot over a bed of white rice (19:20).
