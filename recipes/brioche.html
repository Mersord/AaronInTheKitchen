About these measurements

Metric is the default. US cups are 236.588 mL, not 250 mL. Flour, sugar and other supported dry ingredients use ingredient-specific weight conversions; they are not treated as water. Displayed conversions are rounded.

Where a reliable weight-to-volume conversion is unavailable, weighed ingredients use ounces or pounds in the US view; spoon-measured ingredients use millilitres in the metric view. Ounces (oz) mean weight, not fluid ounces. Counts, pinches, and “to taste” remain unchanged.

For baking and very small quantities, a kitchen scale is more reliable than estimated cups. Changing units does not adjust the recipe for a fan oven or a different pan.

Notes from this source recipe

  • The exported ingredient list omits the main-dough ingredients, although several amounts appear in the instructions. The butter quantity is not stated. Those omissions are not filled in with invented amounts.

For the Tangzhong (starter paste):

  • Strong flour (bread flour) : 50 g ( 0:34 )
  • All-purpose flour : 10 g ( 0.34 )
  • Milk (your choice): 120 g ( 0:34 )

For the gilding:

  • Egg : 1 beaten egg ( 15:06 )

Step-by-Step Cooking Instructions

Step 1: Tangzhong Preparation

  • In a small saucepan, mix the 50 g of strong flour, the 10 g of regular flour and the 120 g of milk ( 0:34 ) .
  • Heat over low heat, stirring constantly, until a thick paste/glue consistency is achieved ( 0:19 ) . Let cool.

Step 2: Mixing the main dough

  • In the bowl of your stand mixer, break the 8 eggs ( 1:08 ) .
  • Add the salt (13 g) ( 1:18 ) , the cooled Tangzhong ( 0:19 ) , the strong flour (500 g) ( 2:46 ) and the classic flour (90 g) ( 4:33 ) .
  • Add the dry yeast (14 g) ( 5:19 ) , the milk (80 g) ( 5:27 ) and the sugar (200 g) ( 7:01 ) .
  • Using the dough hook, start mixing slowly (speed 3 on a KitchenAid) ( 7:38 ) .
  • Let the robot run for 10 to 15 minutes until the dough begins to homogenize and form an elastic gluten network ( 7:50 ) .

3. Incorporating the butter and the first rise

  • While the mixer is running, gradually incorporate the softened butter, piece by piece ( 8:14 ) . Wait until each piece is fully incorporated before adding the next ( 8:27 ) .
  • Once all the butter has been incorporated, cover the bowl with plastic wrap ( 9:05 ) .
  • Let the dough rest and rise at room temperature for 2 hours ( 8:57 ) .

Step 4: Shaping the balls

  • Lightly oil your work surface or pastry mat ( 9:31 ) .
  • Deflate the dough by lifting and firmly folding it over several times ( 10:29 ) . Brioche dough is very sticky, this is perfectly normal ( 10:19 ) . Sprinkle with a little all-purpose flour if it is too difficult to handle ( 11:28 ) .
  • Using a pastry cutter, divide the dough in two (to make two cake tins) ( 1:25 ) .
  • Next, divide each half into several pieces ( 12:55 ) . Form balls of different sizes with the palm of your hand using a circular motion ( 13:20 ) .
  • Place the balls of dough side by side in your cake tins ( 12:35 ) .

Step 5: Cooking

  • Preheat your oven to 160 °C ( 14:56 ) .
  • Generously brush the tops of your brioches with the beaten egg to obtain a beautiful golden crust ( 15:06 ) .
  • Place in the oven and let it cook for approximately 25 minutes ( 15:16 ) .
  • Allow to cool slightly before slicing or enjoying in detachable pieces ( 13:34 ) .
