About these measurements

Metric is the default. US cups are 236.588 mL, not 250 mL. Flour, sugar and other supported dry ingredients use ingredient-specific weight conversions; they are not treated as water. Displayed conversions are rounded.

Where a reliable weight-to-volume conversion is unavailable, weighed ingredients use ounces or pounds in the US view; spoon-measured ingredients use millilitres in the metric view. Ounces (oz) mean weight, not fluid ounces. Counts, pinches, and “to taste” remain unchanged.

For baking and very small quantities, a kitchen scale is more reliable than estimated cups. Changing units does not adjust the recipe for a fan oven or a different pan.

Ingredient conversion reference · Conversion notes

Notes from this source recipe

  • Source gives both mass and volume: 60 grams (Approximately 2 teaspoons). The metric quantity is retained; no water-density assumption is made.

The Dry Ingredients Foundation

  • All-purpose flour: 330 g
  • Baking powder: 12 g
  • Fine sea salt: 6 g

The Sugars & Wet Binders

  • White granulated sugar: 100 g
  • Brown sugar: 110 g
  • Unsalted butter: 113 g
  • Heavy cream: 300 mL (Roughly one standard full container)
  • Sour cream or Greek yogurt: 60 g
  • Large eggs: 2 fresh eggs
  • Vanilla paste: 15 mL (Or 10 mL of high-quality extract)
  • Maple syrup: 80–100 mL (The chef's custom secret ingredient for moisture)

The Mix-In & Garnish

Chopped chocolate bars or chunks: 355 mL total

Step-by-Step Cooking Instructions

1. Set Up Your Baking Station

  • Grab your digital kitchen scale and place your primary mixing bowl directly on top. Utilizing a scale allows you to accurately measure out all your core components into a single container.
  • Remember to zero out or tare your kitchen scale each time you prepare to weigh a new addition.
  • Set your kitchen oven or countertop mini oven to preheat to a steady baking temperature floor of 215–218 °C (218.3 °C).

2. Compile the Dry Foundation and Sugars

  • Weigh out and add 330 g of all-purpose flour directly into your mixing bowl.
  • Add 110 g of brown sugar along with 100 g of regular white granulated sugar directly into the flour. Note: These are breakfast style muffins, but if you prefer to transform this recipe into chocolate cupcakes, simply double the total volume of sugar.
  • Add 5 mL of salt and 12 g of baking powder over the top of the flour.

3. Add the Wet Ingredients and Flavor Enhancers

  • Pour 300 mL of heavy cream and 60 g of sour cream or Greek yogurt straight into the bowl.
  • Add 113 g of your unsalted butter.
  • Stream in 100 mL of maple syrup. Including the syrup ensures an incredibly moist crumb structure, though it can technically be omitted if preferred.
  • Crack your 2 large eggs cleanly into the mix. Baker's Tip: If you are worried about dropping shells or hitting a rare bad egg that could ruin the entire base, crack them into a separate small cup first before dumping them in.
  • Measure out and add 15 mL of vanilla paste.

4. Incorporate the Chocolate Chunks

  • Dump 237 mL and a half of your coarsely chopped chocolate chunks directly into the bowl.
  • Intentionally reserve a modest handful of the smaller chocolate chips or shards on your cutting board to use as a top garnish later.
  • Texture Note: Try to chop your chocolate bars reasonably fine; leaving the chocolate pieces excessively large will force your mechanical mixer blades to work significantly harder to push through the dough.

5. Mix and Scoop the Batter

  • Turn on your hand mixer or stand mixer onto a low speed setting.
  • Mix the ingredients steadily just until everything is completely incorporated into a cohesive, thick batter. Avoid over-agitating the dough so the muffins stay perfectly light and fluffy.
  • Set up your baking trays, utilizing non-stick silicone muffin pans or metal tins lined with paper cups.
  • Use a cookie scoop or large spoon to cleanly transfer the thick chocolate batter, dropping even mounds into each individual muffin cavity.
  • Grab your reserved pile of chocolate shards and scatter them generously over the exposed top surfaces of the raw batter mounds to create a beautiful bakery appearance.

6. Bake and Serve

  • Slide the filled muffin trays carefully onto the center rack of your preheated oven.
  • Bake at 215–218 °C (218.3 °C).
  • Check for absolute doneness; the muffins are perfectly cooked through when the sponge bounces back lightly and the internal structure is set.
  • Chef's Error Alert: Be aware that adding large, heavy chunks of raw chocolate directly to the crown of a muffin means those top surface pieces can occasionally get a bit too dark or caramelized in a hot oven. If this happens, you can pick those dark surface flecks right off, or enjoy them as-is for a rich, toasted s'mores-like accent.
  • Remove the trays from the oven and let them drop temperature slightly. Serve warm while the interior chocolate chunks are still molten, gooey, and melted.
